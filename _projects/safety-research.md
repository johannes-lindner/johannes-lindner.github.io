A key research field is the quantification of road users interactions at complex intersections. The work is based on the following dissertation: __Yiru Jiao (2025)__ ([doi](https://doi.org/10.4233/uuid:ba7621cb-babf-42d0-85d6-a3ef97d3e0af)). The author investigates a data-driven methodology to quantify collision risk in complex multi-directional urban traffic interactions by developing a context-aware, scalable framework that uses coordinate transformations, probabilistic modeling, and self-supervised learning to enable proactive interventions without relying on crash labels. 
My research aims to generalize this approach, moving from car-to-car interaction to interaction of multiple road users groups and considering different road infrastructure types. 

The following screenshot of a Sumonity simulation illustrates the interaction area and proximity resistance field of road users to another. Depending on model parameters and calibration the metric can be used for relative safety evaluations. It can however also be inferred for road user behavior models.
