Project Details

Computational Modeling of Pedestrians in Automated Vehicle Interaction

  • Johannes Lindner
  • PhD Research
  • 2021 - 2025

Project Overview

This PhD research focuses on understanding and modeling how pedestrians interact with and perceive automated vehicles in urban traffic environments. Using sub-microscopic simulation tools, we investigate the behavioral patterns of pedestrians when encountering autonomous vehicles, with particular emphasis on safety considerations and user acceptance.

Research Questions

  • How do pedestrians perceive and react to automated vehicles?
  • What are the safety implications of pedestrian-AV interactions?
  • How can realistic pedestrian models improve AV validation?
  • What are the social and psychological factors influencing these interactions?

Methodology

The research employs:

  • Sub-microscopic Simulation: Detailed modeling of individual pedestrians and their behaviors
  • Empirical Studies: Field observations and laboratory experiments with human subjects
  • Human-in-the-Loop Simulation: Testing pedestrian reactions in controlled simulator environments
  • Agent-Based Modeling: Developing realistic behavior models based on psychological theories

Key Contributions

This research contributes to the development of the SUMONITY co-simulation framework with enhanced pedestrian modeling capabilities. The findings inform standards for automated vehicle safety testing and provide insights for infrastructure design in cities with autonomous vehicles.

Expected Outcomes

  • Validated pedestrian behavior models for traffic simulation
  • Guidelines for safe pedestrian-AV interaction design
  • Improved testing methodologies for autonomous vehicle validation
  • Publications in peer-reviewed traffic safety and transportation research journals
