summary: >
  This study examines how human drivers respond to pedestrian crossings under three visibility conditions—unobstructed, partially obstructed, and fully obstructed—to inform a safety baseline for automated vehicles. In a driving simulator study with 37 participants, driving behavior, physiological responses, and subjective workload were measured. Scenario complexity significantly affected speed, braking, throttle, and subjective workload, but not pupil dilation, gaze entropy, or objective workload; younger and less experienced drivers reacted more strongly.
