summary: >
  This study examines whether a smartphone-based bicycle human-machine interface (HMI) that adds traffic-scenario warnings to routing apps changes cyclists' behavior around autonomous vehicles (AVs). Behavior was analyzed in a coupled Bicycle-AV-Simulator study focusing on speed. Cyclists reduced speed after receiving app information, though the reduction diminished across scenarios, indicating a learning effect; speed also decreased when the AV had priority and was maintained or increased when the cyclist had priority.
