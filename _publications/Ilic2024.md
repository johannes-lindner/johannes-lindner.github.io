summary: >
  This study examines how automated vehicle (AV) communication concepts affect pedestrians' perceived trust at urban intersections. Two virtual reality studies tested driving behavior with and without a visible driver, and vehicle- versus infrastructure-based explicit communication with and without traffic lights. Trust did not differ with or without a driver (4.7 vs. 4.6), but vehicle-based explicit communication raised trust (mean 4.9), while traffic lights had opposite effects depending on communication type.
