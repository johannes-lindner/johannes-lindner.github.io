A few weeks ago, our colleagues Johannes Lindner and Mathias Pechinger presented at the Münchner GI-Runde 2025, a forum dedicated to fostering collaboration between science, industry, and public authorities in the field of geoinformatics.
 
A key question we posed during our presentation was: Why are we, from the Chair of Traffic Engineering and Control, involved in this forum?
 
In the domains of digital twin creation for human-in-the-loop simulator applications and microscopic traffic simulation, we identified numerous opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration. Specifically, for our Open-Source Simulation Framework, Sumonity, the use of open standards such as CityGML is crucial for the automated creation of realistic environments.
We are pleased to collaborate with the team at the TUM Chair of Geoinformatics in the Connected Urban Twins and TUM2TWIN projects, as well as future initiatives to come.
 
Thank you to Prof. Thomas H. Kolbe and Dr.-Ing. Andreas Donaubauer from TUM Chair of Geoinformatics for the invitation to speak and for organizing such an insightful event!
